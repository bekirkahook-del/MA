How to get into consulting from Amherst

Updated Nov 16, 2026 · M&A Prep

Consulting firms like McKinsey, BCG and Bain use case interviews to see how you think. You get a business problem, ask clarifying questions, lay out a structure, work through data and math, then give a recommendation.

The clubs and programs below come from our Amherst guide; the case approach comes from our consulting guides.

The path from Amherst

WhenWhat to do
Learn the formatA case is a business problem, clarifying questions, a structure, data and math, then a recommendation.
Practice structuresUse MECE (mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive) structures, like profit = revenue − costs.
Practice math and sizingTalk through case math out loud and do market sizing (guesstimate) questions top-down or bottom-up.
Prepare your fit storyHave specific answers to "Why consulting?" and the usual behavioral questions.

What consulting interviews test

What to expect

  • Case structure: MECE frameworks such as profitability and market entry.
  • Case math done quickly and out loud.
  • A clear recommendation: answer first, then reasons, risks and next steps.
  • Fit: specific reasons for consulting (variety of problems, learning fast, impact), tied to your own experience, plus a firm-specific reason.

Common questions

How do I prepare for consulting recruiting at Amherst?
Practice cases out loud with a partner, learn MECE structures and case math, and prepare a specific "Why consulting?" answer. The clubs and programs above are a good place to find practice partners.
What is a MECE structure?
MECE means mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive: your categories don't overlap and together they cover the whole problem.
How should I end a case?
Give the answer first, then the reasons, the risks and next steps.
