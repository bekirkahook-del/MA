Behavioral and fit questions for finance interviews

Fit questions decide more offers than people think. Interviewers want a believable story: where you started, what got you interested, what you've done about it and why this role is the next step.

Prepare about five flexible stories (leadership, teamwork, conflict, failure and a challenge) using STAR: situation, task, action, result. Spend most of the time on what you did, and end with a concrete result.

Behavioral questions

Basic

Walk me through your resume.

A strong answer covers
  • Clear arc: start, spark for finance, actions taken, why this role now
  • Highlights 2–3 experiences rather than every line
  • About 90 seconds to 2 minutes
  • Ends on why this specific role
Basic

Why investment banking?

A strong answer covers
  • Specific reasons: learning curve, deal exposure, skills, people
  • Backed by real experiences such as conversations or a stock pitch
  • Avoids money and prestige as the main reasons
  • Shows understanding of what the job actually is
Basic

Why our firm?

A strong answer covers
  • Names specific people they spoke with
  • References specific deals or groups at the firm
  • Connects the firm's strengths to their own goals
  • Avoids generic praise like 'great culture'
Basic

What's your greatest strength?

A strong answer covers
  • One clear strength relevant to banking
  • Backed by a specific example
  • Shows impact or result
  • Concise, under a minute
Basic

Why are you interested in the specific group you're applying to (an industry or product group)?

A strong answer covers
  • Names the group's focus and recent deals
  • Connects it to their own interests or experience
  • Mentions people they spoke with in the group
  • Shows they understand the day-to-day work
Intermediate

Tell me about a time you failed.

A strong answer covers
  • A real failure, not a humblebrag
  • Takes ownership without blaming others
  • Explains what they changed afterward
  • Uses STAR with a concrete result
Intermediate

Tell me about a time you disagreed with a teammate.

A strong answer covers
  • Specific situation with real stakes
  • Shows listening and respect for the other view
  • Explains how it was resolved
  • Positive outcome and what they learned
Intermediate

What's your greatest weakness?

A strong answer covers
  • A real, relevant weakness
  • Not a disguised strength
  • Concrete steps they're taking to improve
  • Evidence of progress
Intermediate

Tell me about a time you led a team.

A strong answer covers
  • Clear role and goal
  • Specific actions they took as leader
  • How they handled a problem or conflict
  • Measurable result
Intermediate

Why an independent advisory firm instead of a bulge bracket?

A strong answer covers
  • Advice-only model with no lending conflicts
  • More senior exposure and responsibility on lean deal teams
  • M&A-heavy deal flow and strong exit opportunities
  • Specific people and deals at the firm
Intermediate

Tell me about a time you worked under a tight deadline.

A strong answer covers
  • Specific situation and stakes
  • How they prioritized and planned
  • What they did when things went wrong
  • Result delivered on time, with a lesson
Intermediate

What would you do if your associate gave you feedback you disagreed with?

A strong answer covers
  • Listens fully and asks questions first
  • Implements the feedback if it's a judgment call
  • Raises concerns respectfully with facts
  • Shows coachability and maturity
Advanced

Why should we hire you over candidates with stronger backgrounds?

A strong answer covers
  • Confident but not arrogant
  • Specific evidence of drive, learning speed or results
  • Shows self-awareness about gaps and how they've closed them
  • Ties back to what makes a great analyst
Advanced

At 2am you find a mistake in a model that goes to the client at 8am. What do you do?

A strong answer covers
  • Tells their associate or VP right away
  • Fixes it and checks what else it affects
  • Doesn't hide or quietly patch it
  • Shows judgment, ownership and calm under pressure
Advanced

Tell me about a deal you've followed. Was it a good idea?

A strong answer covers
  • Accurate basics: buyer, target, price, form of payment
  • Strategic rationale and multiple paid
  • A clear opinion with reasons
  • Risks or what could make them wrong
Advanced

Tell me about a time you had to deliver an unpopular recommendation.

A strong answer covers
  • Specific situation with real stakes
  • Analysis behind the recommendation
  • How they communicated it and handled pushback
  • Outcome and what they learned
Advanced

Where do you see yourself in five years?

A strong answer covers
  • Credible path that includes doing this job well
  • Shows ambition without dismissing the role
  • Mentions skills they want to build
  • Avoids saying they plan to leave right away

Quick quiz (12 questions)

The best way to end a resume walk-through is:
  1. A list of your hobbies and weekend activities
  2. Why you want this specific role now ✓
  3. Your GPA and the classes you liked most
  4. Where you went to high school and why
Close the loop on why you're in the room.
In a resume walk-through, how many experiences should you highlight?
  1. Every line on the resume
  2. 2 to 3 that show initiative ✓
  3. Only your most recent job
  4. None, just talk about goals
Depth beats a list.
How long should your answer to 'Walk me through your resume' be?
  1. About 20 seconds, as short as possible
  2. 90 seconds to 2 minutes ✓
  3. About 5 minutes, covering every detail
  4. About 10 minutes, with a full life story
Long enough for a story, short enough to keep attention.
Which is the weakest answer to "Why IB?"
  1. I want to learn how companies are valued and bought
  2. The pay is great ✓
  3. I enjoyed pitching stocks and want deal exposure
  4. Alumni described the learning curve and it fits me
Money may be true, but it signals you'll leave when the hours get hard.
A strong answer to "Why our firm?" includes:
  1. The firm's stock price and recent performance
  2. Specific people you talked to and deals they did ✓
  3. That it's the most prestigious name you applied to
  4. That the office is close to your family
Specifics prove you did the work.
In a 'Why investment banking?' answer, each reason you give should be backed by:
  1. A statistic
  2. A specific experience ✓
  3. A quote from a CEO
  4. Nothing, keep it short
Stories make motivation believable.
Which is the worst way to answer "Tell me about a failure"?
  1. A real setback you owned and learned from
  2. "I just work too hard" ✓
  3. A group project that went wrong and what you changed
  4. Missing a goal and how you adjusted
A humblebrag dodges the question and interviewers see through it.
Which part of STAR should get the most time?
  1. Situation
  2. Task
  3. Action ✓
  4. Result
Interviewers want to know what YOU did.
For behavioral interviews, how many core stories should you prepare?
  1. One
  2. About five ✓
  3. Twenty
  4. None, improvise
Five flexible stories cover nearly every fit question.
Which is the best question to ask an interviewer?
  1. What does your firm actually do day to day?
  2. What's a deal you enjoyed working on and why? ✓
  3. How much do first-year analysts get paid here?
  4. How many hours a week will I be working?
It's personal, shows interest, and gets them talking.
For "What's your greatest weakness?" you should give:
  1. A strength disguised as a weakness
  2. A real weakness and how you're fixing it ✓
  3. No answer
  4. Something unrelated to work
Self-awareness plus action is the point.
You blank on a fit question mid-interview. The best recovery is:
  1. Fill the silence with whatever comes to mind
  2. Pause, ask for a moment, then answer with a structure ✓
  3. Apologize and say you didn't prepare for that one
  4. Ask the interviewer for a different question
A short pause and a structured answer look composed, not unprepared.
