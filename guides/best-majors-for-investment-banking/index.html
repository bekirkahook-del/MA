Best majors for investment banking

Updated Nov 17, 2026 · M&A Prep

Many students worry they picked the wrong major. The good news is that banks care more about your grades, experience and interview skills than the name of your major.

Here is how the common majors compare and how to fill any gaps.

Short answer

Banks hire from many majors. Finance, economics and accounting are the most common, but engineering, math, computer science and humanities majors also get hired. What matters most is strong grades, proof of interest in finance and the ability to handle the technical interview.

The common majors

MajorWhy it helpsWhat to add
FinanceDirect overlap with valuation and marketsReal experience (clubs, internships) so you stand out from other finance majors
AccountingStrong grasp of the three statements, which every interview testsValuation and deal knowledge
EconomicsGood analytical training and a common banking majorAccounting and modeling skills
Engineering, math or computer scienceShows strong quantitative abilitySelf-taught accounting and valuation, plus finance clubs
Humanities and other majorsShows writing and communication strengthAccounting, valuation basics and clear proof of interest in finance

What matters more than the major

Four things

  • Grades: a strong GPA helps your resume get through the first screen
  • Experience: clubs, student investment funds, internships or related projects
  • Networking: conversations with people who work in banking
  • Technical skills: accounting, valuation, DCF and LBO knowledge

If your major isn't finance

Take an accounting class if you can, join a finance or investment club, learn the basics on your own, and be ready to explain why banking makes sense for you. A clear story matters more than the course list on your transcript.

Common questions

Do I need a finance degree to work in investment banking?
No. Many bankers studied other subjects. You do need to learn the finance fundamentals on your own if your classes don't cover them.
Is a double major worth it?
Only if you want it for its own sake. Spending your time on grades, clubs and networking usually helps more.
What about a business minor?
It can help, but it isn't required. Accounting and corporate finance classes are the most useful ones.
