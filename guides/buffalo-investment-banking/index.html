How to get into investment banking from University at Buffalo

Updated Nov 13, 2026 · M&A Prep

The University at Buffalo School of Management runs a real-money student fund, the Terese Kelly Investment Group, set up in 2011.

UB says alumni of the group have gone to firms like Goldman Sachs, BlackRock, Bloomberg, Citi and Wells Fargo.

Programs and clubs to know

Real money

Terese Kelly Investment Group (TKIG)

  • A student-run endowment fund owned by the Finance Department. UB says the money is real.
  • Split into sectors, each led by a student sector portfolio manager. Members pitch ideas to a panel of peers and professionals.
  • Members visit financial hubs like New York City, Boston and Chicago.
Research

Fisher Family Fellows (F3)

  • A student equity research program sponsored by alum Gregg S. Fisher, started in 2025.
  • Students research small- and mid-cap companies and share their work with Quent Capital.
  • A related group, the Rising Bulls Equity Group, also sits under TKIG.

Your timeline at University at Buffalo

WhenWhat to do
Freshman fallJoin 1–2 finance clubs. Keep your GPA high (many programs want 3.5+, some 3.7+).
Freshman spring–summerLearn accounting and valuation basics. Start 1–2 alumni coffee chats a month. Apply to early-insight programs.
Sophomore fall–springApply to your school's selective IB program. Main window for junior-summer IB internships: applications, interviews and Superdays (recent cycles opened as early as sophomore fall).
Junior summerThe internship that usually turns into your full-time offer.

Recruiting runs about 1.5–2 years ahead of the internship, so the work starts earlier than most students expect. See the full recruiting timeline.

How to stand out

What actually moves the needle

  • Join TKIG and pitch stocks. A Goldman Sachs alum told UB in 2014 that hands-on fund experience helps in interviews.
  • Go on the New York trips to meet School of Management alumni at finance firms.
  • Nail the technicals: the three statements, enterprise value, DCF and a paper LBO. Practice them out loud.
  • Network early with alumni from your school at banks. A warm intro often decides who gets the first interview.
  • Have a tight 60-second story for "walk me through your resume" and "why investment banking."
