How to get into private equity from Colby

Updated Nov 16, 2026 · M&A Prep

A private equity (PE) firm raises money from big investors, uses it to buy companies, usually owns each one for about 3 to 7 years, and sells it for a profit. Most people get in by starting in investment banking for about 2 years, then interviewing for PE associate jobs.

So the path from Colby usually runs through banking first. The clubs and programs below come from our Colby guide, and the steps and interview topics come from our PE guides.

The path from Colby

WhenWhat to do
Freshman–sophomore yearJoin a finance club, learn accounting and valuation basics, and keep your GPA high.
Sophomore–junior yearRecruit for an investment banking internship. This is the usual first step toward PE.
Analyst years (about 2)Work on deals in banking and build modeling skills.
PE associate recruitingInterview for associate roles at funds: paper LBOs, modeling tests, case studies and "would you invest?".

What PE interviews test

What to expect

  • How a fund works: general partners (GPs), limited partners (LPs), the "2 and 20" fees, carry and the hurdle.
  • Paper LBOs and timed modeling tests, including IRR vs. MOIC. MOIC is total money back divided by money in; IRR is the annualized return that accounts for timing.
  • Investment judgment: the market, business quality, returns (entry price, leverage, value-creation plan, exit) and what could go wrong.
  • A case study or "pick a public company you'd take private" question.

Common questions

Can I get into private equity from Colby?
Most people start in investment banking for about 2 years and then interview for PE associate jobs, so the first goal is a banking internship. See the Colby IB guide linked above for the clubs and programs that feed that step.
Is private equity the same as venture capital?
No. Venture capital invests in young startups. Buyout funds, the biggest part of PE, usually buy established, profitable companies.
What is a leveraged buyout?
An LBO is when a PE firm buys a company using a lot of borrowed money. More in what is an LBO.
