Consulting case interviews: structure, math and recommendations

Consulting firms like McKinsey, BCG and Bain use case interviews to see how you think. You get a business problem, ask clarifying questions, lay out a structure, work through data and math, then give a recommendation.

Strong candidates use structures that are MECE (mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive), talk through their math out loud, and finish with the answer first, followed by reasons, risks and next steps.

Case and fit questions

Basic

Why consulting?

A strong answer covers
  • Specific reasons tied to the work: variety of problems, learning fast, impact
  • Connects to their own experience
  • Shows they understand the lifestyle and travel
  • Mentions a firm-specific reason
Basic

A company's profits fell this year. How would you structure the problem?

A strong answer covers
  • Profit = revenue − costs
  • Revenue = price × volume; costs = fixed + variable
  • Compare over time and against competitors
  • Plans to dig into the biggest driver first
Intermediate

A coffee chain's profits are down 20% while revenue is flat. Walk me through how you'd find out why.

A strong answer covers
  • Revenue flat, so costs must be rising
  • Splits costs into fixed (rent, labor) and variable (beans, cups)
  • Asks for data over time and versus peers
  • Forms a hypothesis, like bean prices or wages, and tests it
Intermediate

Our client makes electric scooters and wants to enter Europe. What would you want to know?

A strong answer covers
  • Market size and growth in Europe
  • Competitors and their share; regulations
  • Client's capabilities, costs and how it would enter (build, partner, buy)
  • Financials: investment, break-even, expected return
Intermediate

Fixed costs are $2M a year, price is $40 and variable cost is $15 per unit. How many units to break even? What would you do if we can only sell 60,000?

A strong answer covers
  • Break-even = $2M ÷ ($40 − $15) = 80,000 units
  • 60,000 units means a loss of about $500K
  • Options: raise price, cut variable cost, cut fixed cost, or sell more
  • Recommends the most realistic lever with reasoning
Intermediate

A grocery chain's market share is falling. How would you figure out why?

A strong answer covers
  • Define the market and compare with competitors
  • Split by region, store format and customer segment
  • Look at price, assortment, convenience and online
  • Form and test hypotheses with data
Intermediate

Our client, a hotel chain, wants to raise revenue per room. What would you look at?

A strong answer covers
  • Revenue per available room = occupancy × average daily rate
  • Pricing strategy and demand by season
  • Channel mix and booking fees
  • Ancillary revenue like food, parking and upgrades
Advanced

A private equity firm asks whether it should buy a regional gym chain. How would you approach it?

A strong answer covers
  • Market attractiveness: size, growth, competition (budget gyms, boutiques)
  • Target's position: membership trends, churn, pricing, margins
  • Value creation plan and exit options
  • Risks: economic slowdown, rising rent, at-home fitness
Advanced

An airline wants to raise profits by 10% in two years. Lay out your plan.

A strong answer covers
  • Revenue levers: pricing, fees, routes, load factor
  • Cost levers: fuel hedging, fleet, labor productivity
  • Prioritizes by size of impact and feasibility
  • Gives a clear recommendation with risks
Advanced

Our client, a hospital, has rising wait times in its emergency room. What would you look at?

A strong answer covers
  • Maps the patient flow: arrival, triage, treatment, discharge
  • Finds the bottleneck with data (staffing, beds, tests)
  • Compares demand by hour with staffing
  • Recommends targeted fixes and how to measure success
Advanced

A pharma company is deciding whether to launch a new drug. How would you structure it?

A strong answer covers
  • Market size: patients, diagnosis rate, price
  • Competition and expected share
  • Costs: production, marketing, remaining trials
  • Profitability, risks and a clear recommendation
Advanced

Give me three ways to cut costs at a manufacturing plant by 10%, and how you'd prioritize them.

A strong answer covers
  • Splits costs into materials, labor and overhead
  • Specific levers: procurement, automation, scheduling, energy
  • Prioritizes by size, speed and risk
  • Mentions how to measure results

Quick quiz (12 questions)

MECE stands for:
  1. Most efficient, cheapest estimate
  2. Mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive ✓
  3. Mutually efficient, cost-effective estimates
  4. Margin, equity, cash, earnings
A MECE structure has no overlaps and no gaps.
What should you do first in a case interview?
  1. Give your final answer right away
  2. Clarify the question and goal ✓
  3. Start running calculations immediately
  4. Ask what the salary is for the role
Make sure you're solving the right problem before you structure it.
In an interviewer-led case, the interviewer:
  1. Stays silent the whole time
  2. Guides you through specific questions ✓
  3. Only asks behavioral questions
  4. Lets you pick the industry
Interviewer-led cases follow a set path of questions.
What does 'mutually exclusive' mean in MECE?
  1. No overlap between buckets ✓
  2. Nothing is missing from the buckets
  3. All buckets are the same size
  4. There are only two buckets
Each item fits in one bucket.
What does 'collectively exhaustive' mean in MECE?
  1. Each bucket is worn out from overuse
  2. Together the buckets cover everything ✓
  3. The buckets overlap with each other
  4. Only one bucket is needed
Nothing is left out, so there are no gaps.
Which split of 'customers' is MECE?
  1. Happy and online customers
  2. Young and loyal customers
  3. Big and returning customers
  4. New and existing customers ✓
Every customer is either new or existing, never both.
Revenue is equal to:
  1. Price × volume ✓
  2. Profit − costs
  3. Fixed + variable costs
  4. Volume − price
Revenue is how many units you sell times the price.
Rent for a factory is usually a:
  1. Variable cost
  2. Fixed cost ✓
  3. Revenue driver
  4. Tax
Rent doesn't change with how many units you make.
Fixed costs $100K, price $50, variable cost $30 per unit. Break-even volume?
  1. 2,000 units
  2. 5,000 units ✓
  3. 3,333 units
  4. 10,000 units
$100K ÷ ($50 − $30) = 5,000 units.
Profit equals:
  1. Revenue + costs
  2. Price × costs
  3. Revenue − costs ✓
  4. Volume − price
The core profitability equation.
Which is usually a variable cost?
  1. Office rent
  2. CEO salary
  3. Raw materials per unit ✓
  4. Annual software license fees
Variable costs change with production.
Price $20, variable cost $12. Contribution margin per unit:
  1. $12
  2. $20
  3. $32
  4. $8 ✓
20 − 12 = $8.
