Enterprise value vs. equity value

Updated Nov 3, 2026 · M&A Prep

Equity value is what the shareholders' stake is worth: share price × diluted shares. Enterprise value (EV) is what the whole operating business is worth to everyone who funds it, debt and equity together.

The bridge: EV = equity value + debt + preferred stock + noncontrolling interest − cash. Match multiples to the right numerator: metrics before interest (revenue, EBITDA, EBIT) go with EV; metrics after interest (net income, so P/E) go with equity value.

Enterprise value questions

Basic

What's the difference between enterprise value and equity value?

A strong answer covers
  • Equity value belongs to shareholders: share price × diluted shares
  • Enterprise value is the core business's value to all capital providers
  • EV = equity value + debt + preferred + noncontrolling interest − cash
  • EV pairs with EBITDA and revenue; equity value pairs with net income
Basic

Can enterprise value be negative? What would that mean?

A strong answer covers
  • Yes, if cash exceeds equity value plus debt
  • Usually a struggling company with lots of cash
  • Market expects the business to burn cash
  • Rare and often temporary
Intermediate

Why do we subtract cash when calculating enterprise value?

A strong answer covers
  • Cash isn't part of the core operating business
  • A buyer effectively gets the cash back
  • EV is meant to value operations only
  • Some argue only excess cash should be subtracted
Intermediate

A company issues $100 of stock and uses it to repay $100 of debt. What happens to equity value and enterprise value?

A strong answer covers
  • Equity value rises by $100
  • Debt falls by $100
  • Enterprise value is unchanged
  • Capital structure changes don't change the business's value
Intermediate

Which valuation multiples use enterprise value, and which use equity value?

A strong answer covers
  • EV/Revenue, EV/EBITDA, EV/EBIT use enterprise value
  • P/E and price/book use equity value
  • Match numerator and denominator to the same capital providers
  • Metrics before interest go with EV
Intermediate

A company's share price rises 10%. What happens to equity value and enterprise value?

A strong answer covers
  • Equity value rises 10%
  • Enterprise value rises by the same dollar amount
  • EV rises by less than 10% if the company has net debt
  • Debt and cash don't change
Intermediate

Why is EV/EBITDA used more often than P/E in banking?

A strong answer covers
  • Independent of capital structure
  • Lets you compare companies with different debt levels
  • EBITDA is closer to operating cash flow
  • P/E is affected by interest and one-time items
Advanced

How do you calculate diluted shares using the treasury stock method?

A strong answer covers
  • Include in-the-money options and warrants
  • Assume they're exercised and the company receives the strike proceeds
  • Assume proceeds buy back shares at the current price
  • Net new shares = options − shares repurchased
Advanced

Why do you add noncontrolling interest to enterprise value?

A strong answer covers
  • Parent owns over 50% of a subsidiary and consolidates 100% of its financials
  • EBITDA includes all of the subsidiary's earnings
  • Adding NCI makes EV consistent with the consolidated metrics
  • Same logic as matching numerator and denominator
Advanced

How do you treat convertible bonds when calculating diluted shares and enterprise value?

A strong answer covers
  • If in the money, assume conversion into shares
  • Then remove the bond from debt to avoid double counting
  • If out of the money, keep it as debt
  • Check the conversion price against the share price

Quick quiz (5 questions)

Equity value $500, debt $200, cash $50. Enterprise value is:
  1. $750
  2. $650 ✓
  3. $550
  4. $450
500 + 200 − 50 = 650.
Which multiple pairs with equity value?
  1. EV/EBITDA
  2. EV/Revenue
  3. P/E ✓
  4. EV/EBIT
Net income belongs to equity holders only, so P/E uses equity value.
A company borrows $100 and keeps it all as cash. Enterprise value:
  1. Rises $100
  2. Falls $100
  3. Stays the same ✓
  4. Doubles
Debt +100 and cash +100 cancel out. The business itself didn't change.
Share price $20 and 50 diluted shares. Equity value is:
  1. $1,000 ✓
  2. $70
  3. $2.50
  4. $100
20 × 50 = $1,000.
Which item is subtracted when going from equity value to enterprise value?
  1. Debt
  2. Preferred stock
  3. Cash ✓
  4. Noncontrolling interest
Cash is subtracted; debt, preferred stock and noncontrolling interest are added.
