Free cash flow explained simply

Updated Nov 5, 2026 · M&A Prep

Free cash flow is the cash a company has left after paying to run and maintain the business. It's the money that could go to investors.

Bankers care about it because a company is worth the cash it can produce in the future.

The formulas

Unlevered free cash flow (used in a DCF)

EBIT × (1 − tax rate) + depreciation & amortization − capital expenditures − increase in working capital. It ignores debt, so it's cash available to everyone.

Levered free cash flow

Like unlevered, but after paying interest and debt. It's cash available to shareholders.

Common questions

Why is free cash flow better than net income?
Net income includes non-cash items and skips real spending like equipment. Free cash flow shows actual cash.
Can free cash flow be negative?
Yes, often for fast-growing companies that spend heavily to expand.
