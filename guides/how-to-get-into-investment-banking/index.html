How to get into investment banking: a step-by-step plan

Updated Nov 9, 2026 · M&A Prep

Most investment banking analysts get hired through a summer internship after junior year of college, and that internship usually comes from recruiting that starts much earlier. The people who get in aren't always the smartest. They start early, network a lot and show up to interviews already knowing the technical questions.

Here's the plan, year by year, plus the four things every bank checks.

The 4 things banks look at

1. Grades

A strong GPA gets your resume past the first screen. Many banks look for roughly 3.5 or higher, and a weak GPA is hard to make up for.

2. Relevant experience

Finance clubs, student investment funds, earlier internships (even small local firms), and anything where you worked with numbers or clients.

3. Networking

Talking to people who work at the banks. At many firms, a referral or a good conversation with an analyst is what gets your application read.

4. Interview skills

Knowing the technical questions (accounting, valuation, DCF, LBO) and telling a clear story about why you want banking.

What to do, year by year

WhenFocus on
High schoolGood grades, get into the strongest college you can, learn what banking is, start a stock pitch or investing club
Freshman yearJoin finance clubs, keep a high GPA, learn accounting basics, start talking to upperclassmen in banking
Sophomore yearApply to sophomore programs and early insight weeks, network hard, start learning interview technicals
Junior yearRecruit for the junior summer internship (often starts very early), interview, do well in the internship to get a full-time offer
Senior yearAccept the return offer, or recruit for full-time spots if you didn't get one

Recruiting has moved earlier over the years, and many big banks now interview for junior summer internships more than a year in advance. Check the recruiting timeline guide and each bank's careers page so you don't miss a window.

Common questions

Do I need to go to a target school to get into investment banking?
No, but it's harder from a non-target. Target schools have on-campus recruiting and big alumni networks. From other schools, you make up for it with more networking, strong grades, relevant experience and very good interview prep.
What major should I pick for investment banking?
Finance, economics and accounting are the most common, but banks hire every major. What matters is that you can show interest in finance and handle the technical questions.
When should I start preparing?
Freshman year is ideal. Recruiting for the junior summer internship often starts during sophomore year, so you want grades, clubs and networking in place before then.
What do investment banking interviews ask?
A mix of fit questions (why banking, why this bank, tell me about yourself) and technical questions on accounting, enterprise value, valuation, DCF, LBO and M&A math. Some also ask brain teasers or mental math.
