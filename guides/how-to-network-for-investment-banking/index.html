How to network for investment banking: a simple plan for students

Updated Nov 17, 2026 · M&A Prep

In investment banking, networking is not about collecting contacts. It is about having a few real conversations with people at the firms you want, so that when your application comes in, someone knows your name.

Most students avoid it because it feels awkward. It gets much easier once you have a simple routine, so here is one.

Why networking matters

Banks receive far more applications than they can read closely. A good conversation with an analyst or associate can get your resume noticed, and it also teaches you what the job is really like so your interview answers sound informed.

The 5 steps

Step 1

1. Build a target list

  • Pick 10 to 15 firms across bulge brackets, elite boutiques and middle-market banks
  • For each, list the offices and groups you would consider
  • Start with the firms where you have a real connection
Step 2

2. Find people to contact

  • Your school's alumni database and career center
  • LinkedIn: search the firm name plus your school
  • Student clubs, previous interns and upperclassmen who now work in finance
Step 3

3. Send a short message

Keep it to a few sentences: who you are, what you share (school, club, hometown), and one clear ask for 15 to 20 minutes. Use the template in our cold email guide.

Step 4

4. Run a good call

Prepare questions, listen more than you talk, and thank them for their time. Our coffee chat checklist and question list cover this.

Step 5

5. Follow up

  • Send a thank-you email within a day that mentions something specific they said
  • Log the call in a simple tracker with the date and notes
  • Check in every month or two with a short update, such as a new club role or an interview

A weekly routine that works

DayWhat to do
MondayPick 5 people to contact and send the messages
WednesdayReply to anyone who answered and schedule calls
Thursday or FridayTake your scheduled calls and send thank-you emails
SundayUpdate your tracker and plan next week's list

Mistakes to avoid

Don't

  • Ask for a job or a referral in the first conversation
  • Send the same copy-paste message to everyone without changing a word
  • Ask questions you could answer from the firm's website
  • Disappear after the call without saying thank you

Common questions

How many people should I contact?
There is no magic number. A steady habit, a handful of new messages each week, beats a big burst followed by silence. Expect many people not to reply, and do not take it personally.
Do I need to network if I go to a target school?
It still helps. Target schools have more on-campus events and alumni, but a personal conversation can set you apart even there.
What if I have no finance experience to talk about?
Be honest. Say you are early in your journey and are trying to learn. Clubs, classes, a stock pitch or a simple investing project all give you something real to mention.
When should I start?
Freshman year is a great time, because the pressure is low and people are often happy to help students who are just starting out.
