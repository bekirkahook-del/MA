How to pitch a stock in an interview: a simple 2-minute template

Updated Nov 9, 2026 · M&A Prep

A stock pitch is a short argument for why a stock is priced wrong. Interviewers don't care if you pick a winner. They want to see if you can explain a business clearly, back an opinion with numbers, and think about risk.

This is a simple template that fits in about two minutes.

The 2-minute structure

PartTimeWhat to say
1. Recommendation10 secTicker, buy or sell, and a price target
2. Business overview20 secWhat the company does and how it makes money
3. Thesis45 sec2 to 3 reasons the stock should move
4. Valuation20 secWhy today's price is wrong
5. Catalysts10 secEvents that could make the market notice
6. Risks15 secWhat could prove you wrong

How to do each part

1. Lead with the answer

Start with something like: "I'd buy [Company] with a 12-month price target of $X, about Y% upside." Don't bury it under background.

2. Explain the business simply

Say what it sells, who buys it and how it earns money. If you can't explain it to a friend in two sentences, pick a simpler company.

3. Give two or three thesis points

Make them specific and checkable, like a segment growing faster than expected or margins that should improve because of a cost change. "It's a great company" is not a thesis.

4. Show the valuation

Compare it to something: P/E or EV/EBITDA against similar companies, or a simple DCF. Example: "It trades at 12x earnings while peers trade at 18x, and I think that gap closes because of X." See valuation multiples.

5. Name the catalysts

A catalyst is an event that makes the market change its mind: an earnings report, a product launch, a new CEO, a spin-off. Without one, a cheap stock can stay cheap for years.

6. Be honest about risks

Give two real risks and say what would make you sell. This is what separates strong candidates, because it shows judgment.

Picking the stock

Choose a company you've actually researched. Read the latest annual report (10-K) and earnings call. Avoid the most obvious mega-cap names unless you have a unique angle, since every other candidate pitches them. Refresh your numbers the day before the interview.

Most students pitch a long (buying the stock). A short (betting it falls) can stand out at hedge funds, but it's riskier and harder to defend, so only do it if you really understand the story.

Common mistakes

Mistakes to avoid

  • Pitching a stock because it went up recently
  • No valuation or no price target
  • Saying there are no risks
  • Using numbers you can't explain in a follow-up
  • Rambling past three minutes

Common questions

How long should a stock pitch be?
About two minutes in an interview, with a clear structure: recommendation, business, thesis, valuation, catalysts and risks.
Should I pitch a long or a short?
Most students pitch a long. A short can stand out at hedge funds but is riskier and harder to defend.
Which valuation method should I use?
Keep it simple: P/E or EV/EBITDA against peers, or a basic DCF, and be ready to explain how you got your price target. See DCF interview questions.
What follow-up questions will I get?
Expect "Why now?", "What if interest rates rise?", "What would make you wrong?" and "How does it compare to its competitors?" More in hedge fund interview questions.
