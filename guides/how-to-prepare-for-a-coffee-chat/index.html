How to prepare for a coffee chat: a simple checklist

Updated Nov 17, 2026 · M&A Prep

A coffee chat is short, so the prep matters more than the call. Bankers can tell in the first two minutes whether you did your homework, and a prepared student is much easier to help.

You don't need hours. Here's what to do before, during and after, plus a way to practice the whole thing first.

Before the call: the 5-step prep

Step 1

1. Research the person

  • Read their LinkedIn: school, past internships, current group and how long they've been there
  • Look for something you share, like the same school, club, hometown or major
  • Note one thing about their path you want to ask about
Step 2

2. Research the firm and group

  • Read the firm's careers page and the group's page if it has one
  • Look up one or two recent deals or news items from the firm's press releases
  • Know what kind of firm it is: bulge bracket, elite boutique, middle market or buy side
Step 3

3. Write your 30-second intro

Most chats start with "So tell me about yourself." Have a short answer ready: your school and year, what got you interested in finance, and what you're hoping to learn. See how to answer tell me about yourself.

Step 4

4. Plan 5 to 8 questions

Write more than you need, ordered from most to least important. Mix questions about their path, their day-to-day work and the firm. Pull ideas from our coffee chat questions list, then make each one specific to this person.

Step 5

5. Handle the logistics

  • Confirm the time and time zone, and who calls whom
  • Find a quiet spot with good signal
  • Keep your notes, your questions and a pen in front of you
  • Be ready a few minutes early

A simple intro template

Template

Fill in the blanks

"I'm a [year] at [School] studying [major]. I got interested in finance when [one real reason, like a class, a club or an internship]. Right now I'm [what you're doing: a finance club, a student fund, learning technicals], and I'm trying to learn more about [banking / your group] to figure out where I'd fit best."

Keep it under 30 seconds. The goal is to give them context so the rest of the call is about them, not you.

How the call usually goes

PartWhat happens
StartThank them for their time and keep small talk short
Your introGive your 30-second story when they ask
The middleAsk your questions and follow up on what they say
The closeThank them, ask who else you should talk to, and ask if it's okay to stay in touch

Watch the clock. When the time you asked for is up, offer to let them go. If they want to keep talking, great.

During the call

Do these

  • Listen more than you talk
  • Follow up on their answers instead of jumping to your next question
  • Take short notes so you can mention something specific later
  • Match their energy: if they're busy, keep it tight

Avoid these

  • Asking about pay
  • Asking things you could find on the firm's website
  • Asking for a referral or a job in the first chat
  • Reading your questions like a list

After the call

Send a short thank-you email within a day that mentions one specific thing they told you. Log the chat in a tracker with the date and what you talked about, then check in every month or two with a short update. If they suggested someone else to talk to, reach out to that person and mention who sent you.

Common questions

How long should I prepare for a coffee chat?
For most chats, a focused 30 to 45 minutes is plenty: research the person and firm, write your intro and plan your questions. Spend more time on your first few chats while you build your intro and question list.
Should I send my resume before a coffee chat?
Usually not unless they ask. Have it ready to send if they offer to look at it or pass it along.
What if the conversation stalls?
That's why you write more questions than you need. Go back to something they said earlier and ask them to say more about it, or ask what they wish they'd known before starting.
What should I wear for a virtual coffee chat?
If it's on video, dress neatly, like business casual. If it's a phone call, it doesn't matter, but being in a quiet place does.
