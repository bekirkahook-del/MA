Investment banking exit opportunities: where analysts go next

Updated Nov 17, 2026 · M&A Prep

One reason investment banking is popular is that it can open many doors afterward. The training in modeling, valuation and deals is useful across finance.

Here are the most common next steps and what each one actually involves.

Short answer

After a few years in investment banking, analysts commonly move into private equity, hedge funds, venture capital, corporate development or corporate finance, credit funds and asset management, or go to business school. What is available depends on your bank, your deal experience and your networking.

The main exits

Popular

Private equity

Funds that buy companies, improve them and sell them later. Banking analysts are a common hiring pool, because they already know models and deals. See what is private equity.

Hedge funds

Funds that invest in liquid securities such as stocks and bonds. Roles often value strong analysis and a good investment pitch. See what is a hedge fund.

Venture capital and growth equity

Investing in startups and fast-growing companies. Backgrounds vary, and the work is more about spotting companies and people than financial modeling.

Corporate development and corporate finance

Working inside a company, running acquisitions, financing and planning. Hours are often more predictable than banking.

Credit funds and asset management

Investing in debt or managing portfolios for institutions and individuals.

Business school

Many analysts go to an MBA program, then return to finance or move into another industry.

Startups and other industries

Finance skills are useful for startup founders and operators, so some analysts move into non-finance roles.

What helps you get the exit you want

Practical tips

  • Work on the best deals you can and learn the details of each one
  • Build relationships with people in the field you want, even before you need them
  • Pick a bank and group that fits your goal (for example, a group with a lot of LBO work if you want PE)
  • Practice the interview style of your target field early

Common questions

Is private equity the only good exit?
No. Private equity gets the most attention, but corporate roles, venture capital, credit and business school are all common and well respected.
Do I need to stay in banking for two years?
Many people do, partly because of recruiting timelines and training, but it depends on the field you want and the firm's own rules.
Does the bank I start at matter?
It can, especially for the biggest private equity firms, but your deal experience and relationships matter too.
