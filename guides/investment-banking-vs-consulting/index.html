Investment banking vs. consulting: which one fits you?

Updated Nov 17, 2026 · M&A Prep

Investment banking and consulting are two of the most popular first jobs for ambitious business students. Both are competitive, both involve long hours and steep learning curves, and both are often mentioned in the same breath.

They are different jobs, though. Here is how they compare, so you can decide which one to train for first (or whether to prepare for both).

Short answer

Investment banking is about financial transactions: models, valuation and deals. Consulting is about solving business problems: research, analysis and recommendations. Interviews differ too, with technical finance questions for banking and case interviews for consulting.

The one-line difference

Investment banks help companies raise money and buy, sell or merge with other companies. Consulting firms advise companies on how to run their business better: strategy, operations, growth, cost. Banking is built around financial transactions and models. Consulting is built around solving business problems and presenting recommendations.

Side by side

Investment bankingConsulting
Main workFinancial models, valuation, pitch decks and deal materialsResearch, data analysis and slides that answer a client's business question
Core skillAccounting, valuation and ExcelStructured problem solving, communication and analytics
Typical projectA sale, merger, IPO or financingA strategy, growth, cost or operations study

TravelMostly office-based, deals drive the scheduleOften client-site travel, depending on the firm
HoursLong and unpredictable, tied to deal deadlinesLong, usually more predictable, tied to project deadlines

How the interviews differ

Banking interviews

Expect technical questions on the three statements, enterprise value, valuation, DCF and LBO, plus fit questions like "Why banking?" and "Walk me through your resume."

Consulting interviews

Expect case interviews: you get a business problem, structure it out loud, do quick math and give a recommendation. Fit questions matter too. See our consulting case interview guide.

Where each can lead

Banking analysts often move into private equity, hedge funds, corporate development or business school. Consultants often move into corporate strategy, startups, private equity or business school. Neither path locks you in, and plenty of people move between the two worlds.

How to choose

Lean toward banking if you

  • Like accounting, valuation and building models
  • Want to work on deals and capital markets
  • Are comfortable with a very steep technical learning curve

Lean toward consulting if you

  • Like solving open-ended business problems
  • Enjoy structuring ideas, presenting and talking to clients
  • Want broad exposure to different industries

You can prepare for both in freshman and sophomore year: strong grades, finance or business clubs, and communication skills help for each. Recruiting timelines differ by firm, so check each firm's careers page for current dates.

Common questions

Is investment banking harder to get into than consulting?
Both are competitive and both vary by firm and school. The recruiting process is different, so the better question is which interview you would rather prepare for: technical finance questions or case interviews.
Which pays more, banking or consulting?
Pay depends on the firm, location and level, and it changes from year to year. Check recent figures from the firms you are targeting rather than relying on a general rule.
Can I switch from consulting to banking, or the reverse?
It happens, but it is easier to switch early in your career. Be ready to explain why you want the move and to show the technical skills the new field expects.
Should I apply to both?
Many students do, especially early on. Just make sure you can tell a clear story for each one, because interviewers will ask why you want their particular field.

Practice both: technical finance questions and structured thinking, in short levels you can finish between classes.
