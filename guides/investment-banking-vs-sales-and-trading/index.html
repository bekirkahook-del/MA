Investment banking vs. sales and trading

Updated Nov 17, 2026 · M&A Prep

Banks have two big sides that new students often confuse: investment banking and sales and trading. They sit in the same firm but feel very different day to day.

Here is how they compare.

Short answer

Investment bankers advise companies on deals and financings, working on long projects built around models and presentations. Sales and trading professionals work on a markets desk, helping clients buy and sell securities and managing risk throughout the trading day.

Side by side

Investment bankingSales and trading
Main jobAdvise companies on mergers, sales and financingsExecute and make markets in securities for clients
PaceProject-based, with long deadlines and late nights during dealsFast and tied to market hours
Main toolsExcel and PowerPointTrading systems, market data and quick math
SkillsAccounting, valuation and detail-oriented workMarket knowledge, quick thinking and decision-making under pressure
InterviewsTechnical finance questions and fitMarket questions, mental math, a stock or trade pitch and fit

Sales vs. trading

Sales

Talks with clients, shares market ideas and takes their orders. Relationship skills matter most.

Trading

Manages the firm's positions and prices, and executes trades. Risk management and speed matter most.

How to prepare

For sales and trading, follow the markets daily, practice mental math, and be ready to pitch a trade idea. See sales and trading interview questions. For banking, focus on accounting and valuation (see investment banking interview questions).

Common questions

Which has better work-life balance?
It depends on the group and the firm. Trading hours are tied to the market, and banking hours are tied to deals, so each is demanding in a different way.
Can I apply to both?
Many students do. Just be ready to explain why you want each, because the interviews and the day-to-day work are different.
Do traders need a finance degree?
No, but they need strong math and market interest. Many come from quantitative or business majors.

Practice mental math, market questions and technicals in short levels.
