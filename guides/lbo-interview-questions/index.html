LBO interview questions and a worked paper LBO

In a leveraged buyout, a private equity firm buys a company using a lot of borrowed money. The company's own cash flow pays the debt down, and the sponsor earns its return when it sells. Returns come from three levers: EBITDA growth, multiple expansion and debt paydown.

Worked example: buy $100 of EBITDA at 10x ($1,000), funded with $600 of debt and $400 of equity. In five years EBITDA is $150, you exit at 10x ($1,500), and debt is down to $300. Exit equity is $1,500 − $300 = $1,200, so MOIC is $1,200 ÷ $400 = 3.0x and IRR is about 25%.

LBO and private equity questions

Basic

Walk me through a basic LBO.

A strong answer covers
  • Sponsor buys a company with debt plus equity
  • Sources and uses must balance
  • Cash flow pays down debt over 3–7 years
  • Exit and calculate MOIC and IRR
Basic

Why does a PE firm use leverage?

A strong answer covers
  • Smaller equity check boosts returns
  • Debt is cheaper than equity, and interest is tax-deductible
  • Debt paydown increases equity value
  • Trade-off: more risk of distress
Intermediate

What makes a good LBO candidate?

A strong answer covers
  • Stable, predictable cash flow
  • Low capex and working capital needs
  • Strong market position
  • Room for operational improvement and a clear exit
Intermediate

How do PE firms create value?

A strong answer covers
  • EBITDA growth via revenue and margins
  • Multiple expansion
  • Debt paydown
  • Add-on acquisitions and better governance
Intermediate

Paper LBO: buy $100 EBITDA at 10x with 60% debt. In 5 years EBITDA is $150, exit at 10x, debt paid down to $300. What are MOIC and IRR?

A strong answer covers
  • Entry EV $1,000, debt $600, equity $400
  • Exit EV $1,500, less $300 debt = $1,200 equity
  • MOIC 3.0x
  • IRR about 25%
Advanced

What is a dividend recap, and how does it affect returns?

A strong answer covers
  • Company takes on new debt to pay the sponsor a dividend
  • Returns cash early, which boosts IRR
  • MOIC may not change much
  • Adds risk to the company
Advanced

How do you protect against downside in an LBO?

A strong answer covers
  • Conservative leverage and covenant-light terms
  • Buy at a reasonable multiple
  • Stress-test the model in a downturn
  • Diversified customers and recurring revenue
Advanced

Paper LBO: $50 EBITDA bought at 8x with 50% debt. EBITDA grows to $65 in 5 years, exit at 9x, debt falls to $120. MOIC and IRR?

A strong answer covers
  • Entry EV $400, debt $200, equity $200
  • Exit EV $585, less $120 debt = $465 equity
  • MOIC about 2.3x
  • IRR about 18%
