Market sizing questions, with worked examples

Market sizing questions ask you to estimate a number you can't look up, like how many cups of coffee Americans drink a day. Interviewers grade the process, not the exact answer.

Pick top-down (start from a big number like population and narrow it) or bottom-up (start from one store or customer and scale up). Say your assumptions out loud, round to easy numbers, and sanity-check at the end. Handy anchors: about 330 million people in the U.S. and about 130 million households.

Market sizing practice

Basic

Estimate how many cups of coffee are sold in the U.S. each day.

A strong answer covers
  • Clear top-down or bottom-up structure
  • U.S. population about 330M, share who drink coffee, cups per drinker
  • States assumptions and rounds cleanly
  • Sanity check, answer roughly 300–500M cups a day
Basic

Estimate how many smartphones are sold in the U.S. each year.

A strong answer covers
  • Population about 330M, share with smartphones
  • Replacement cycle of about 2–3 years
  • Clear math with rounded numbers
  • Sanity check, lands roughly in the 100–150M range
Intermediate

How many gas stations are there in the United States?

A strong answer covers
  • Structured approach (cars, fill-ups, station capacity or population per station)
  • States assumptions clearly
  • Lands on a reasonable number (the industry estimate is about 150,000 fueling sites)
  • Sanity-checks the result
Intermediate

How many tennis balls are sold in the U.S. each year?

A strong answer covers
  • Segments buyers (casual players, regular players, clubs, pros)
  • Players × balls per year by segment
  • Clear math with rounded numbers
  • Sanity check against something known
Advanced

Estimate the size of the U.S. college textbook market.

A strong answer covers
  • Students × courses × spending per course
  • States assumptions (about 19M students, rentals and used books)
  • Reasonable final range
  • Sanity check against total spending per student
Advanced

Estimate the annual revenue of a single Starbucks store.

A strong answer covers
  • Customers per hour by time of day
  • Average ticket size
  • Hours open × days a year
  • Sanity check: company-run stores average roughly $2–2.5M a year
