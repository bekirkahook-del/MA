Mental math for finance interviews: fast tricks and practice

Some finance interviews throw in quick math with no calculator: "What's 13% of 87?" or "What's the IRR if you triple your money in 5 years?" They want to see that you can handle numbers under pressure and talk through your thinking.

You don't need to be a genius. A few tricks cover most of it.

The tricks

Trick 1

Percentages: build from 10% and 1%

13% of 87 → 10% is 8.7, 3% is 2.61, total 11.31. Same move works for any percent.

Trick 2

Round, then fix

17 × 24 → 17 × 25 = 425, minus one 17 = 408. 99 × 99 → 9,900 − 99 = 9,801.

Trick 3

Numbers ending in 5, squared

35² → 3 × 4 = 12, then add 25 → 1,225. 65² → 6 × 7 = 42 → 4,225.

Trick 4

Rule of 72

Years to double ≈ 72 ÷ the rate. At 8% a year, money doubles in about 9 years. Flip it for IRR: doubling in 5 years is about 72 ÷ 5 ≈ 14–15%.

Practice questions

What's 15% of 360?
54. 10% is 36, 5% is 18, add them.
What's 25 × 48?
1,200. 25 × 4 = 100, and 48 is 4 × 12, so 100 × 12.
Revenue grows from $80M to $100M. What's the growth rate?
25%. The change is 20, and 20 ÷ 80 = 25%. A common trap is dividing by 100 and saying 20%.
A stock goes up 10%, then down 10%. Where does it end up?
Down 1%. $100 → $110 → $99, because the 10% drop is taken from a bigger number.
What's the angle between the clock hands at 3:15?
7.5 degrees. The minute hand is on the 3, but the hour hand has moved a quarter of the way to the 4 (15 minutes × 0.5° per minute).
You triple your money in 5 years. Roughly what's the IRR?
About 25%. 1.25 to the 5th power is about 3.05.
