How to get into investment banking from Pitt

Updated Nov 12, 2026 · M&A Prep

Pitt students can study finance in the College of Business Administration and join student groups aimed at Wall Street.

One group, Pittsburgh Stop to Wall Street, exists to help Pitt students land investment banking internships and full-time offers.

Programs and clubs to know

IB focus

Pittsburgh Stop to Wall Street

  • A Pitt student group whose mission is to strengthen the pipeline from Pitt to Wall Street.
  • Focuses on investment banking summer internships, usually for junior year, and then full-time offers.

Pitt Business resources

  • The Financial Analysis Lab is a 3,000 square foot room built to look like a trading floor, with 58 computer stations and real-time market data.
  • Mentoring Match connects students with over 500 alumni volunteers, many in finance and investing.

Your timeline at Pitt

WhenWhat to do
Freshman fallJoin 1–2 finance clubs. Keep your GPA high (many programs want 3.5+, some 3.7+).
Freshman spring–summerLearn accounting and valuation basics. Start 1–2 alumni coffee chats a month. Apply to early-insight programs.
Sophomore fall–springApply to your school's selective IB program. Main window for junior-summer IB internships: applications, interviews and Superdays (recent cycles opened as early as sophomore fall).
Junior summerThe internship that usually turns into your full-time offer.

Recruiting runs about 1.5–2 years ahead of the internship, so the work starts earlier than most students expect. See the full recruiting timeline.

How to stand out

What actually moves the needle

  • Join Pittsburgh Stop to Wall Street early and use its members to learn the recruiting calendar.
  • Pittsburgh is home to PNC, so practice networking locally, then reach out to Pitt alumni in New York.
  • Nail the technicals: the three statements, enterprise value, DCF and a paper LBO. Practice them out loud.
  • Network early with alumni from your school at banks. A warm intro often decides who gets the first interview.
  • Have a tight 60-second story for "walk me through your resume" and "why investment banking."
