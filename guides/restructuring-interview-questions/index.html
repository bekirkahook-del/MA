Restructuring interview questions

Restructuring (RX) bankers advise companies, or their creditors, when a company has more debt than it can handle. Interviews test how bankruptcy works, who gets paid in what order, and how to value a distressed business.

Learn the capital stack (secured debt, then unsecured, then equity), how to run a recovery waterfall and why the fulcrum security, the class where value runs out, usually ends up owning the company.

Restructuring questions

Basic

What's the difference between Chapter 7 and Chapter 11 bankruptcy?

A strong answer covers
  • Chapter 7: liquidation
  • Chapter 11: reorganization while operating
  • Chapter 11 features: automatic stay, DIP financing, plan of reorganization
  • Creditors paid by priority
Basic

What signs would tell you a company is heading toward distress?

A strong answer covers
  • Rising debt/EBITDA and falling margins
  • Shrinking cash and liquidity
  • Large debt maturities coming due
  • Bonds trading well below face value
Intermediate

Walk me through a typical capital structure and who gets paid first.

A strong answer covers
  • Secured debt (first lien, then second lien)
  • Unsecured bonds, then subordinated debt
  • Preferred stock, then common equity
  • Absolute priority rule
Intermediate

What is a 363 sale, and why might a company use one in Chapter 11?

A strong answer covers
  • A court-approved sale of assets under Section 363 of the Bankruptcy Code
  • Buyers get the assets free and clear of most claims
  • Often faster than a full plan of reorganization
  • Usually starts with a stalking-horse bid, then an auction
  • Sale proceeds then go to creditors in priority order
Intermediate

What's the difference between a liquidity problem and a solvency problem?

A strong answer covers
  • Liquidity: not enough cash for near-term obligations
  • Solvency: liabilities exceed asset value
  • A company can be solvent but illiquid
  • Different fixes: bridge financing vs. restructuring
Intermediate

A company has $500M of debt maturing in 9 months and can't refinance. What are its options?

A strong answer covers
  • Amend and extend with existing lenders
  • Distressed exchange or debt-for-equity swap
  • Asset sales or new money (rescue financing)
  • Chapter 11 if out-of-court options fail
Intermediate

Debtor-side vs. creditor-side advisory: what's the difference in your day-to-day work?

A strong answer covers
  • Debtor side advises the company on its options and the plan
  • Creditor side advises lenders on maximizing recovery
  • Both build liquidity and recovery models
  • Negotiation positions differ on valuation
Intermediate

What is a prepackaged Chapter 11?

A strong answer covers
  • Company negotiates the plan with creditors before filing
  • Votes are gathered in advance
  • Can exit bankruptcy in weeks instead of months
  • Lower cost and less disruption
Intermediate

What's the difference between secured and unsecured debt in a bankruptcy?

A strong answer covers
  • Secured debt is backed by specific collateral
  • Secured creditors recover first from that collateral
  • Unsecured creditors share what's left
  • Unsecured recoveries are usually much lower
Advanced

How would you value a distressed company?

A strong answer covers
  • Use DCF and multiples but on normalized or restructured numbers
  • Consider liquidation value as a floor
  • Value the enterprise, then run the recovery waterfall
  • Adjust for higher risk and uncertainty
Advanced

EV is $500, secured debt $300, unsecured $400. Walk through recoveries and what unsecured holders might negotiate for.

A strong answer covers
  • Secured recovers 100% ($300)
  • Unsecured gets $200, a 50% recovery
  • Equity gets nothing
  • Unsecured may push for equity in the reorganized company or argue for a higher valuation
Advanced

What is a liability management exercise, and why do lenders fight over them?

A strong answer covers
  • Out-of-court moves like uptiers or drop-downs to raise new financing
  • Can let some lenders jump ahead of others in priority
  • Excluded lenders lose value, leading to lawsuits
  • Lenders now sign cooperation agreements to resist
Advanced

Why would creditors argue for a lower valuation in Chapter 11, and equity for a higher one?

A strong answer covers
  • Valuation decides who gets the equity in the reorganized company
  • Lower value means senior creditors take more of the equity
  • Higher value leaves room for junior claims and old equity
  • Each side's expert valuation reflects its incentives
Advanced

What is the fulcrum security and why does it matter?

A strong answer covers
  • The class where enterprise value runs out
  • Partially repaid in the waterfall
  • Usually receives most of the reorganized equity
  • Investors buy it to gain control of the company
Advanced

A company has $900 of debt and $90 of EBITDA, with a $300 maturity next year. What would you recommend?

A strong answer covers
  • Leverage is 10x, too high to refinance easily
  • Explore amend and extend or an exchange first
  • Consider asset sales or new money
  • Prepare a Chapter 11 path if lenders won't agree

Quick quiz (14 questions)

Who gets paid first in a bankruptcy?
  1. Common shareholders
  2. Unsecured bondholders
  3. Secured lenders ✓
  4. Preferred shareholders
Secured lenders have claims on specific assets and sit at the top.
The absolute priority rule says:
  1. Everyone takes the same percentage loss
  2. Senior claims are paid in full before junior claims get anything ✓
  3. Junior claims are paid first because they took the most risk, then seniors split whatever is left
  4. The judge picks which creditors get paid
Payment flows down the stack in order.
Enterprise value is $500. Secured debt of $300 is paid in full, and unsecured bonds are owed $400. The unsecured recovery is:
  1. 100%
  2. 75%
  3. 50% ✓
  4. 0%
$500 − $300 secured = $200 left for $400 of unsecured claims: 200 ÷ 400 = 50%.
In bankruptcy, which claim ranks highest in priority?
  1. Unsecured bonds
  2. First-lien secured debt ✓
  3. Preferred stock
  4. Common equity
Secured first-lien lenders are paid first.
In bankruptcy priority, which claim ranks lowest?
  1. Second lien
  2. Subordinated debt
  3. Common equity ✓
  4. Preferred stock
Common equity is last.
What does 'subordinated' debt mean?
  1. It's paid before senior debt
  2. It charges no interest at all
  3. It's secured by the company's assets
  4. It's paid after senior debt ✓
Subordinated holders accept lower priority for higher yield.
Chapter 7 bankruptcy means:
  1. Reorganization
  2. Liquidation ✓
  3. A debt refinance
  4. An IPO
The business shuts down and assets are sold.
In bankruptcy, the automatic stay:
  1. Freezes creditor collection efforts ✓
  2. Pays employees before anyone else
  3. Cancels all of the company's contracts
  4. Forces the company to sell its assets
It gives the company breathing room to negotiate.
Why does DIP financing get super-priority?
  1. The SEC requires it in every bankruptcy
  2. It convinces lenders to fund a bankrupt company ✓
  3. It's always the cheapest debt available
  4. Shareholders demand it before filing
Without top priority, no one would lend into a bankruptcy.
Chapter 11 bankruptcy is mainly a:
  1. Liquidation
  2. A merger
  3. Reorganization ✓
  4. An IPO
The company keeps operating while it restructures.
Enterprise value is $600. First lien is owed $400, second lien $300 and unsecured $250. Which class is the fulcrum security?
  1. First lien
  2. Second lien ✓
  3. Unsecured
  4. Equity
Value runs out inside the second lien, so it's partly repaid and likely gets the equity.
Enterprise value is $600. First lien is owed $400 and second lien $300. What does the second lien recover?
  1. 100%
  2. 67% ✓
  3. 50%
  4. 0%
After the $400 first lien, $200 is left for the $300 second lien: 200 ÷ 300 ≈ 67%.
The best-interests test compares each creditor's recovery with:
  1. The original amount of the loan
  2. What it would get in a Chapter 7 liquidation ✓
  3. The company's current stock price
  4. What creditors got at other companies
A plan can't leave a dissenting creditor worse off than liquidation.
What is the fulcrum security?
  1. The most senior secured debt
  2. The class where value runs out, usually getting the new equity ✓
  3. The most junior class of debt, which is always wiped out and receives nothing in a restructuring plan
  4. The company's cash on hand
It's partly repaid and converts to ownership.
