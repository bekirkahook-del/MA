The investment banking Superday: what to expect and how to prepare

Updated Nov 5, 2026 · M&A Prep

A Superday is the final round of investment banking recruiting. You do several back-to-back interviews in one day, usually with bankers from analyst up to managing director. Offers often come within a few days, sometimes the same night.

It's less about one perfect answer and more about being consistent: sharp on technicals, clear on why you want the job, and easy to talk to for six hours straight.

What the day looks like

The format

  • Usually 3 to 6 interviews, about 30 minutes each
  • In person at the office or on video
  • Mix of junior bankers (more technical) and senior bankers (more fit)
  • Some banks add a case study, modeling test or group exercise

Who interviews you

  • Analysts and associates: technical questions, "would I want to work with you at 2am?"
  • VPs and MDs: your story, why banking, why this firm, how you'd handle clients

The questions that come up most

Walk me through your resume.
Keep it to about 2 minutes. Go in order, explain each choice in one line, and end with why that path leads to banking and this firm.
Why investment banking?
Give 2 or 3 specific reasons tied to your experience: the learning curve, working on live deals, the people you met while networking. Avoid leading with money. See the full guide to this answer.
Why our bank?
Mention real things: deals they worked on, the group you'd join, and specific people you talked to and what stood out.
Walk me through the three financial statements.
Income statement shows profit, balance sheet shows what the company owns and owes at a point in time, cash flow statement shows how cash moved. Net income links the income statement to the cash flow statement, and ending cash links the cash flow statement to the balance sheet.
Walk me through a DCF.
Project unlevered free cash flow for 5 to 10 years, calculate terminal value, discount everything back at WACC to get enterprise value, then bridge to equity value. More in the DCF guide.
Tell me about a deal you've been following.
Pick one recent deal you actually understand: who bought whom, the price and multiple, why the buyer did it, and one risk. Keep it to 2 minutes.

The week-before checklist

Do these

  • Practice your story out loud until it's under 2 minutes
  • Drill technicals daily: accounting, EV vs. equity value, DCF, LBO, merger math
  • Know 2 recent deals and 1 deal the bank worked on
  • Prepare 2 or 3 questions for each interviewer
  • Send short thank-you notes after
