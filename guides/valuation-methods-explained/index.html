Valuation methods explained: comps, precedents and DCF

Updated Nov 17, 2026 · M&A Prep

Valuation answers a simple question: what is a company worth? There is no single right answer, so bankers use several methods and compare them.

These three methods show up in almost every banking interview.

Short answer

Bankers usually value a company three ways: trading comparables (what similar public companies are worth), precedent transactions (what similar companies sold for) and a discounted cash flow (what its future cash flows are worth today). They compare the three results to form a valuation range.

The three methods

Market-based

1. Trading comparables (comps)

  • Find similar public companies
  • Look at multiples such as EV/EBITDA or P/E
  • Apply a sensible multiple to your company's own numbers
Deal-based

2. Precedent transactions

  • Find similar companies that were recently bought or sold
  • Look at the multiples paid in those deals
  • Apply them to your company; these often include a premium for control
Intrinsic

3. Discounted cash flow (DCF)

  • Forecast the company's free cash flow
  • Estimate a value for the years beyond the forecast (terminal value)
  • Discount everything back to today using the cost of capital

Which one gives the highest value?

Precedent transactions often come out higher than trading comps, because buyers usually pay a premium to gain control. A DCF depends heavily on your assumptions, so small changes in growth or discount rate can move the answer a lot.

Putting it together: the football field

Bankers show the results of each method as a range on a single chart called a football field. The overlap between ranges helps the client see a reasonable price range.

Pros and cons

MethodStrengthWeakness
CompsBased on what the market is paying todayHard to find perfect comparables, and markets can be too high or low

Common questions

Which valuation method is best?
None on its own. Bankers use all three and compare, because each has different strengths and weaknesses.
Why do precedent transactions include a premium?
Buyers often pay extra to take control of a company, so deal prices tend to be higher than where the stock trades day to day.
What is an LBO analysis used for in valuation?
It estimates how much a financial buyer could pay and still earn its target return, which helps set a floor on the price. See what is an LBO.
