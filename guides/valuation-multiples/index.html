Valuation multiples explained: EV/EBITDA, P/E and more

Updated Nov 5, 2026 · M&A Prep

A multiple compares a company's value to one of its numbers, like profit. It's how bankers quickly say whether something is cheap or expensive.

If similar companies sell for 10x their EBITDA, a company with $50M of EBITDA might be worth about $500M.

The main multiples

MultipleValue ÷Good for
EV / EBITDAEnterprise value ÷ EBITDAMost companies; ignores debt differences
EV / RevenueEnterprise value ÷ salesFast-growing companies with little or no profit
P / EShare price ÷ earnings per shareMature companies, banks

Rule to remember: enterprise value goes with numbers that belong to everyone (revenue, EBITDA). Equity value goes with numbers that belong to shareholders (net income).

Common questions

What is a good EV/EBITDA multiple?
It depends on the industry. Fast-growing tech companies trade at much higher multiples than slow-growing industries.
Why can't you use EV / net income?
Net income is after interest, so it only belongs to shareholders. It doesn't match enterprise value, which includes debt holders.
What are trading comps?
Comparing a company to similar public companies using these multiples.
