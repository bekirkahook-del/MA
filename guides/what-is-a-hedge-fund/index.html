What is a hedge fund? Explained simply

A hedge fund is an investment fund that pools money from wealthy people and institutions and tries to earn strong returns in any market, up or down.

Unlike a normal mutual fund, hedge funds can bet on stocks falling (short selling), use borrowed money and trade almost anything.

Common strategies

StrategyWhat it means
Long/short equityBuy stocks you think will rise, short ones you think will fall
Global macroBet on big trends like interest rates, currencies and countries
Event-drivenTrade around events like mergers or bankruptcies
QuantUse computer models and data to find trades

Common questions

How do hedge funds make money?
They charge a management fee on the money they run and keep a share of the profits they make for investors.
What is the difference between a hedge fund and a mutual fund?
Mutual funds are open to everyone and mostly just buy stocks and bonds. Hedge funds are for wealthy investors and use more strategies, like shorting and leverage.
How do you get a job at a hedge fund?
Common paths are investment banking, equity research or trading. Being able to pitch a stock well is key.
