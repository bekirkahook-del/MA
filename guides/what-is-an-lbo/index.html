What is an LBO? A simple explanation with an example

A leveraged buyout (LBO) is when an investor, usually a private equity firm, buys a company using mostly borrowed money. The company's own cash flow pays down that debt over time.

It's like buying a rental house with a mortgage: the rent pays the loan, and when you sell, you keep the gain.

A simple example

StepNumbers
Buy the company$100M price: $40M from the PE firm, $60M borrowed
Own it for 5 yearsProfits grow and $30M of debt gets paid off
Sell the companySells for $130M
Pay off the rest of the debt$130M − $30M remaining debt = $100M to the PE firm
Result$40M became $100M: 2.5x the money, about a 20% yearly return

Common questions

Why do private equity firms use so much debt?
Debt means the firm puts in less of its own money, so the same profit is a bigger return on what it invested.
What makes a good LBO candidate?
Steady cash flow, low need for big spending, a strong market position and room to improve.
What is the risk of an LBO?
If the business struggles, the debt payments can become too much, and the company could go bankrupt.
