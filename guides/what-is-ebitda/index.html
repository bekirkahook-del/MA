What is EBITDA? Simple definition and example

Updated Nov 5, 2026 · M&A Prep

EBITDA stands for Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation and Amortization. It's a quick way to measure how much money a company's core business makes.

It ignores how the company is financed (interest), taxes, and non-cash costs (depreciation and amortization), so it's easier to compare companies.

How to calculate it

Formula

EBITDA = Net income + Interest + Taxes + Depreciation + Amortization. Or: Operating income (EBIT) + Depreciation + Amortization.

Common questions

Why do investment bankers use EBITDA?
It's a simple way to compare the core profits of different companies, and it's used in the popular EV/EBITDA valuation multiple.
What is wrong with EBITDA?
It ignores real costs like buying equipment (capital expenditures) and changes in working capital, so it can make a company look healthier than its actual cash flow.
Is EBITDA the same as cash flow?
No. Cash flow also subtracts things like capital expenditures, taxes and working capital changes.
