What is investment banking? Explained simply

Updated Nov 12, 2026 · M&A Prep

Investment bankers help companies with big money moves: buying or selling another company (M&A), raising money by selling stock (like an IPO), or borrowing money by selling bonds. Companies hire banks for these deals because they're rare, complicated and huge.

Bankers get paid a fee when the deal closes, which is why the job is so intense and so well paid.

What bankers actually do

Advisory

Mergers and acquisitions (M&A)

Advising a company that wants to buy another company, or helping an owner sell. This is the most famous part of banking.

Financing

Raising money (capital markets)

Helping companies sell stock (equity capital markets, including IPOs) or bonds and loans (debt capital markets).

Advisory

Restructuring

Helping companies that are in financial trouble fix their debt, sometimes in bankruptcy.

Coverage groups vs. product groups

Banks split teams two ways. Coverage groups focus on an industry, like healthcare, technology or energy. Product groups focus on a type of deal, like M&A, leveraged finance or restructuring. They work together on deals.

Common questions

Is investment banking hard to get into?
Yes. Top banks get far more applicants than spots, so grades, networking and interview prep all matter. Starting early helps a lot.
What hours do investment bankers work?
Long ones. Junior bankers often work 70 to 90 hours a week, more during busy deals. Many banks have added rules to protect weekends, but it's still one of the most demanding jobs out of college.
What do investment bankers do after a few years?
Many move to private equity, hedge funds, corporate development at a company, or startups. Others stay in banking and get promoted.
Is investment banking the same as being a stockbroker?
No. Stockbrokers and financial advisors help people invest their own money. Investment bankers advise companies on deals and raising money.
