What is M&A (mergers and acquisitions)? Explained simply

Updated Nov 17, 2026 · M&A Prep

M&A stands for mergers and acquisitions: the buying, selling and combining of companies. When you read that one company is buying another, that is an M&A deal.

Investment banks earn fees by advising on these deals, which is why M&A is one of the most talked-about areas of banking. Here are the basics.

Merger vs. acquisition

TermWhat it means
AcquisitionOne company buys another. The buyer is the acquirer and the company being bought is the target.
MergerTwo companies combine into one new company, usually as roughly equal partners.
DivestitureA company sells a division or business unit to someone else.
Management buyoutThe company's own managers buy it, often with outside financing.

In everyday conversation people say "merger" for almost any deal, even when one side is clearly buying the other.

Why companies do deals

Common reasons

  • Grow faster by buying customers, products or technology
  • Enter a new market or country
  • Cut costs by combining operations (these savings are called synergies)
  • Remove a competitor
  • Sell a business that no longer fits the strategy

How a typical sale process works

Step 1

1. Preparation

The seller hires an investment bank. The bank collects financials, builds models and writes marketing materials such as a short teaser and a longer information memorandum.

Step 2

2. Marketing

The bank contacts potential buyers, signs confidentiality agreements and sends them the materials. Interested buyers submit first offers.

Step 3

3. Diligence and negotiation

A smaller group of buyers studies the company in detail, then submits improved offers. The seller negotiates price and terms with the most attractive bidder.

Step 4

4. Signing and closing

The two sides sign a purchase agreement. After approvals are obtained, the deal closes and payment is made.

What bankers do on a deal

Analysts build financial models and valuation analyses (see valuation multiples and enterprise value vs. equity value), prepare pitch decks and marketing documents, and track the process. More senior bankers talk with clients and negotiate. See what analysts actually do.

How buyers decide what to pay

Buyers look at what similar companies trade at, what similar deals have sold for, and what the company's future cash flows are worth. They also check whether the deal helps or hurts their own earnings per share, which is called accretion and dilution.

Common questions

Is M&A the same as investment banking?
No. Investment banking is the broader business. M&A advisory is one of its main services, alongside raising money for companies through debt and equity offerings.
Who pays the bank in an M&A deal?
Typically the client pays advisory fees, and a large part of the fee is often paid only if the deal actually closes.
What is a synergy?
A benefit that exists only because two companies combine, such as eliminating duplicate costs or selling more products to the same customers.
