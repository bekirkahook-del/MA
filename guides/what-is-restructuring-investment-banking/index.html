What is restructuring in investment banking?

Updated Nov 17, 2026 · M&A Prep

Restructuring is the part of banking that helps companies in financial distress. It is less well known than M&A, but it is a respected niche and a common interview topic.

Here is what it involves.

Short answer

Restructuring bankers advise companies in financial trouble, and their creditors, on how to fix a balance sheet or reorganize debt. The work includes negotiating with lenders, out-of-court deals and bankruptcy cases such as Chapter 11, which lets a company reorganize while it keeps operating.

What restructuring bankers do

The work

  • Analyze a company's cash, debt and options
  • Advise on how to change or reduce debt
  • Negotiate with lenders and other creditors
  • Help run a sale or a bankruptcy process if needed

Who the clients are

ClientWhat they want
The distressed company (debtor)To stay in business and fix its balance sheet
Lenders and bondholders (creditors)To get back as much of their money as possible
Potential buyers or investorsTo buy assets or take part in a turnaround at a good price

Chapter 11 vs. Chapter 7

Chapter 11Chapter 7
PurposeReorganize the company so it can keep operatingShut the company down and sell its assets
Who runs itUsually the existing management, with court oversightA court-appointed trustee
OutcomeOften a new capital structure and a continuing businessProceeds go to creditors by priority

The idea of priority

When a company can't pay everyone, creditors are paid in a set order. Secured lenders are generally ahead of unsecured creditors, who are ahead of shareholders. Restructuring interviews often test this order, sometimes through a recovery waterfall (see restructuring interview questions).

Is it a good career?

Restructuring work tends to pick up when the economy is under stress, and the deals are often complex and fast-moving. People in this field often learn a lot about debt and legal structure, which is helpful for credit investing and private equity too.

Common questions

Do I need to know law?
No, but you need to understand the basics of bankruptcy and how debt is ranked. Lawyers handle the legal side.
What skills do I need?
Strong accounting, a good understanding of debt, comfort with models and the ability to communicate clearly under pressure.
Is restructuring only busy in recessions?
Activity usually rises in tough times, but companies also restructure debt for other reasons, so the work is not limited to recessions.
