## 1. Why do IB interviewers care so much about whether they can spend long hours with you, and how can you show you'll be enjoyable to work with?
https://www.quora.com/answer

Because analysts often work 70–80+ hour weeks in a small team. The person interviewing you might be sitting next to you at 1am fixing a pitch deck. Most candidates who reach a Superday can already answer the technicals, so the deciding question becomes "would I want this person on my team at 1am?"

This is often called the "airport test": would I be fine stuck in an airport with this person for 6 hours?

How to show it:

- **Be a normal human in the first 2 minutes.** Small talk counts. Ask about their group or their week, and actually listen.
- **Have a real "tell me about yourself" story.** Keep it under 60 seconds, specific, and not a resume read-out.
- **Show you take feedback well.** If they correct you on a technical, say "that makes sense, thanks," not a defensive re-explanation.
- **Talk about teams, not just results.** Give one example of grinding through something hard with other people (a club, a job, a sport).
- **Be curious.** Ask 1–2 good questions about their deals or how the team works.
- **Don't fake it.** Interviewers talk to dozens of people and spot a rehearsed personality fast.

Technicals get you in the room. Being easy to work with gets you the offer.

## 2. Tips for staying relaxed and confident during high-pressure IB interviews?

Most nerves come from not knowing what's coming. The fix is reps, not a mindset trick.

1. **Practice the top 50 questions out loud, not just in your head.** Walk me through the 3 statements, how a DCF works, EV vs equity value, a paper LBO. Saying them out loud is a different skill than reading them.
2. **Do 3–5 mock interviews** with friends, club members or alumni. The first one is always rough. That's the point.
3. **Have your story locked:** "walk me through your resume," "why banking," "why this bank." These come up in nearly every interview, so there's no excuse to wing them.
4. **Use a buffer line when you blank:** "Let me think through that for a second." Then talk through your logic. Interviewers care about how you think more than a perfect answer.
5. **Slow down.** Nervous people talk fast. Pause before you answer.
6. **Remember they want to fill the seat.** It's not a trap. They're hoping you're the one.

I built a free site that turns this practice into 5-minute levels (mnaprep.com) because doing reps was the only thing that helped me feel ready.

## 3. Alternatives for investment bankers who want to earn less (better hours) but see few options outside high-paying finance?

There are more exits than it looks like from inside the bank. Common ones, roughly from most to least "finance-y":

| Path | Why people go | Hours (general) |
|---|---|---|
| Corporate development / strategy | Same M&A skills, inside one company | Usually much lighter |
| FP&A / corporate finance | Modeling and planning, predictable schedule | Light |
| Growth equity / smaller PE funds | Investing, often less brutal than mega-fund PE | Medium |
| Startup finance / operations | Ownership, equity, building something | Varies a lot |
| Commercial banking / credit | Lending analysis, steadier pace | Lighter |
| Consulting | Strategy work, more variety | Medium–heavy |
| Business school → career switch | Clean reset into a new industry | — |

The IB skill set (modeling, valuation, writing, handling pressure) transfers well. The pay cut is real, but so is getting your evenings back.

## 4. Is an Accounting degree good enough to land a job in investment banking?

Yes. Accounting is one of the best majors for IB, because a big chunk of the technical interview is accounting: the 3 statements, how they link, and how changes flow through.

What actually decides it isn't the major:

- **GPA:** aim for 3.5+. Many banks screen on it first.
- **Recruiting early:** junior-summer internships are the main path in, and recruiting for them often starts sophomore year.
- **Networking:** alumni at banks, coffee chats, warm intros.
- **Finance experience:** finance clubs and any finance-related internship.
- **Valuation skills:** accounting majors usually know the statements cold but need to add DCF, comps and LBO basics.

If you're an accounting major, your edge is the technicals. Spend your extra time on networking and valuation.
