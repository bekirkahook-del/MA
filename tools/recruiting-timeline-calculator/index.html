How investment banking recruiting works, year by year

Most students land a full-time investment banking job through a junior-summer internship. Banks hire their summer analysts early, often a year and a half before the internship starts, so the real work happens in your first two years of college.

Freshman year: build the base

Join one or two finance clubs, keep your GPA high (many banks screen at 3.5), and learn accounting basics: how the income statement, balance sheet and cash flow statement connect. Apply to early-insight and diversity programs. Some of them feed straight into internship interviews.

Sophomore year: the main window

Applications for junior-summer internships at many large banks open during sophomore year. That's when you send applications, do online assessments and HireVues, and go to first rounds and Superdays. Networking with alumni before applications open matters a lot, because a referral often decides who gets an interview.

Junior summer: the internship

You work 10 weeks as a summer analyst. Strong interns usually get a return offer for a full-time analyst job after graduation.

Missed the window?

You still have options: smaller banks and boutiques that recruit later, off-cycle internships, a related role (Big 4 transaction services, valuation, corporate banking) and lateral moves, or a master's in finance.

Common questions

When should I start recruiting for investment banking?
Freshman year for clubs and basics. The main applications for junior-summer internships now happen during sophomore year (recent cycles opened as early as sophomore fall), about 1.5–2 years before the internship.
Why is banking recruiting so early?
Banks compete for the same students, so they keep moving dates earlier. That's why sophomores now interview for internships that start the summer after junior year.
Do these dates apply to every firm?
No. They're typical timing. Each bank sets its own dates, so check careers pages and set alerts.
